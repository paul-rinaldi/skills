# Market Check

Based on Alex Hormozi's *$100M Offers*. The idea: a great offer can't rescue a bad market, so choose the market before writing the offer.

## Step 1: Name the market
Start from one of the three big markets: **health**, **wealth**, or **relationships**. Then narrow it to a specific group of people, defined by who they are *and* the situation they're in. "Small businesses" is too broad. "Solo physical therapists opening their first clinic" is about right.

## Step 2: Score it on four tests (1–5 each)
1. **Pain:** Is this a problem they urgently want solved, or just a nice-to-have? Look for evidence: complaints, workarounds, money already being spent.
2. **Purchasing power:** Can they actually pay? If the user and the payer are different people (say, an employer, an agency, or a grant), score the payer.
3. **Easy to reach:** Is there a list, community, event, publication, or association where they gather?
4. **Growing:** Is the group or its spending expanding, flat, or shrinking?

Any score of 1 or 2 is a red flag. Say which test failed and whether narrowing the niche or switching to a different payer fixes it.

## Step 3: Commit, then niche down
- Recommend one niche and explain why. Resist the pull to serve everyone.
- Check whether the niche can bear a premium price. Narrower niches with acute pain usually can.
- If two niches are close, propose a cheap test: a few customer conversations in each, or a small outreach test.

## Output
- A scoring table with one line of evidence per test, marking each as researched or guessed
- The recommended niche, stated in one sentence
- Red flags, and what would change the verdict

Then hand off to **grand-slam-offer-builder**. To find Milwaukee groups where the niche gathers, use **milwaukee-tech-ecosystem**.
