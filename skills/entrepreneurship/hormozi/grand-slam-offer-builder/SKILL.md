# Grand Slam Offer Builder

Based on Alex Hormozi's *$100M Offers*. The goal is an offer so complete that comparing it to alternatives feels pointless, which lets you charge on value instead of competing on price.

Before starting, you need a chosen niche (from **market-check**) and the dream outcome in the customer's own words.

## Step 1: Map the journey and list every obstacle
Walk through what the customer does before, during, and after getting the result. At each step, list everything that could stop or frustrate them. Aim for 15–30 problems. More is better here. Tag each obstacle with the Value Equation lever it hurts:
- It isn't the result they want (dream outcome)
- They don't believe it'll work (likelihood)
- It takes too long (time)
- It's too hard or costly to do (effort)

## Step 2: Turn each problem into a solution
Rephrase each obstacle as "How to [solve it] even if [their objection]." Keep one solution per problem.

## Step 3: Choose delivery for each solution
Brainstorm several ways to deliver each solution:
- 1-on-1, small group, or one-to-many
- Live or recorded
- Done-for-you, done-with-you, or do-it-yourself
- Format: call, template, tool, checklist, software, community, and so on

Note what each option costs you to deliver.

## Step 4: Trim and stack
- **Cut** options that are expensive to deliver and add little value.
- **Keep** options that are cheap to deliver and high value. These are the gold.
- **Keep selectively** options that are expensive but high value, and price them in.
- **Stack** what's left into one named offer, and show the value of each piece next to the price.

## Output
- A problem-to-solution table showing the lever hit, the delivery chosen, and the cost to deliver
- The final stack, with each component and its value
- A suggested price and the reasoning behind it
- The one-sentence promise

Then use **offer-enhancers** for the wrapper (bonuses, guarantee, urgency, name), and **value-equation-audit** to score the result. For a hackathon or MVP, mark which stack items are faked or manual for the first test; **mvp-experiment-designer** helps with that.
