# Problem Worth Solving

Source method: FOR-M Workshop 2. The point is to spend real time in the problem space before building. Skipping it is the easiest way to fail.

## Step 1: Problem-space research (diverge, then converge)
Work through four lenses. Ask the user one lens at a time, and fill gaps with web research where it helps.
1. **Internal: who am I?** What skills, access, and unfair advantages does the team have?
2. **Market: what's the space like?** Size, trends, and recent changes.
3. **Competition: who else is out there?** Direct rivals, workarounds, and the status quo.
4. **Customer: who am I helping?** A specific segment, not "everyone."

Expect guessing, checking, and rewriting. Flag any lens that is still a guess.

## Step 2: Write the problem statement
Use this format: **How might we** [action] **for** [specific customer segment] **so that** [desired outcome]?

Example: "How might we increase communication for families of elderly so that they can keep up-to-date on in-home recovery?"

Draft 3 variants that differ in segment or outcome. Then pressure-test each one:
- Is the segment specific enough to find 5 real people in it this week?
- Is the outcome observable?
- Does the action leave room for more than one solution?

Help the user pick one.

## Step 3: Turn it into a story
Use four beats, one or two sentences each:
1. **Set the scene.** What's the market like?
2. **Bring in characters.** Who's your customer? Name a persona.
3. **Create a conflict.** What problem do they have?
4. **Offer a resolution.** What's your solution?

## Output
Deliver these three things:
- The research notes, grouped by lens
- The chosen HMW statement, with the alternates listed
- The four-beat story, ready to read aloud in under 60 seconds
